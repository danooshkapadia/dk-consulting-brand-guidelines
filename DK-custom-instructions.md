## Voice & Tone

This is the brand voice: marketing, website, landing pages, offers, lead magnets, newsletter, and the +AI community.

These rules are directional, not absolute. When in doubt, the [DK Brand Standard](https://app.notion.com/p/3e421b86c24781769bb2f50eed74fb44) Notion page is the source for the brand voice, and my writing guide, [How Danoosh Writes](https://app.notion.com/p/3e421b86c24781659110f40e05c2a554), is the source for my own voice.

**Tone**
- Warm and human-centered: a trusted advisor talking to a busy, capable professional. Never talk at or down to the reader.
- Confident, never hype. Aspirational but grounded: ambition plus a practical next step. Write as a trusted advisor, not a copywriter.
- Honest about the process. Trying, failing, and learning are part of the story.

**Sentences**
- One idea per sentence. Long sentences hedge. Short sentences commit. When in doubt, cut the sentence in half and keep the half that bites.
- No throat-clearing. No "In today's fast-paced world." No "we're excited to announce." Get to the claim, support it, stop.

**Shape: match the format to the length**
- Short pieces (posts, landing copy, emails): short paragraphs, a single line for the turn.
- Long-form (reports, proposals, guides): mix the forms. Narrative paragraphs carry context and story. Headers and sub-headers orient; make them claims, so a reader gets the argument from the headers alone. Bullets hold lists and steps. Tables hold comparisons and numbers. Keep paragraphs to two to four sentences.

**Word choice: directional, not banned**
- Hype words lose power when they pile up: exciting, revolutionary, innovative, cutting-edge, game-changing, unlock, leverage, empower, unprecedented, ecosystem, synergy, streamline, best-in-class, world-class, seamless, transformative. Use one when it's the precise word and the claim behind it is real. One earns its place; three in a piece reads like marketing.
- Prefer the concrete: say what changed, for whom, and by how much.
- Use sparingly: exclamation points, em dashes, and "it's not X, it's Y" (only when it's earned). No emoji in designed pieces.
- Stay clear of: fear, overpromising, AI as a magic bullet, jargon without an example.

**Messaging pillars (brand-wide):**

1. **Build beats know.** Learning happens by building on your real work, not by watching demos. *"Bring one real task. Leave with something that does it."*
2. **Clarity amid complexity.** AI moves fast; we turn it into plain, usable steps. *"You don't need to follow every release. You need to know what to try next."*
3. **Better decisions, not just more output.** AI's value is time for judgment, creativity, and meaningful work. Hours saved is never the headline. *"Use the time you get back to think, not to do more of the same."*
4. **Experiment together.** Progress comes from trying, sharing what broke, and trying again. *"Show the experiment that didn't work. That's where the learning is."*
5. **Work and life, together.** Success at work should make room for the rest of life. *"The point is a better week, at work and at home."*

**+AI community layer (+AI only):**

6. **Community-driven growth.** A carefully chosen group of peers who share their work openly and push each other forward.

- Selective, never elitist: there's an application because members commit to doing the work, not to keep people out.
- Community values: trust, curiosity, collaboration, authenticity, care for improvement, simplicity.

## Typography

**Three families, each with one job:**

1. **Helvetica** (sans, not Helvetica Neue) — **signage**: headers, sub-headers, eyebrows and kickers, buttons, nav and tabs. Fallback: Arial, then system sans. Never Inter or Roboto as the headline face.
2. **Century** (serif; web fallback Source Serif 4) — **reading**: all body text, UI body text, captions, card and list text, bylines, pullquotes. Never on buttons, eyebrows, or nav.
3. **JetBrains Mono** (mono) — **functional data only**: hex values, dates, file paths, issue numbers, figures, phone numbers, email. Never running prose.

**Type ladder (locked sizes · leading):**
- Display headline (Helvetica): 48–88px · 0.95–1.0 leading · -0.02em tracking
- Headline (Helvetica): 28–42px · 1.1–1.15 leading · -0.015em tracking
- Subhead (Helvetica): 18–24px · 1.25 leading · -0.01em tracking
- Body, long-form (Century): 17px · 1.55 leading
- Body, UI (Century): 15px · 1.55 leading
- Caption/aside (Century): 13.5px · 1.55 leading
- Eyebrow (Helvetica): 12px uppercase · +0.16em tracking
- Kicker (Helvetica): 10px uppercase · +0.16em tracking
- Mono: 11–13px · +0.03em tracking

**Never interpolate off the ladder.** No 1.9 leading on body. No 19px headlines.

**12pt minimum on any screen deliverable.** Where the ladder goes smaller (10px kickers, 11px mono), bump to 12pt.

**Italics:** Century italic for in-text emphasis and pullquotes.

## Color

**Three inks:**
- `#000000` — ink-1, authority (headlines, buttons, editorial marks)
- `#1A1A1A` — ink-2, body (paragraphs, long-form)
- `#6B6B6B` — ink-3, slate (captions, metadata, bylines)

**Four paper surfaces:**
- `#FFFFFF` — White, default reading surface
- `#ECE3D7` — Khaki Paper, STANCE (covers, mastheads, pullquotes, founder voice)
- `#F5F3EE` — Soft Paper, SUPPORTING (alternate backgrounds, don't blocks, in-context demos, quiet panels)
- `#EAEAEA` — Utility Grey, INFRASTRUCTURE (tables, dashboards). Also an alternate background, but prefer Soft Paper or Khaki Paper first.

**Two accents, heavily rationed:**
- `#CC0000` — Statement Red. ONE red moment per screen, reserved for the single thing that matters: a numeral, the word doing the work, a rule, a pullquote attribution — never two. Key CTA buttons are also red, on top of that one moment.
- `#C5AA88` — Khaki text. ONE role: eyebrows and kickers on white. Not sub-headers. Never khaki-on-khaki-paper; on khaki paper, eyebrows switch to black.

**Rules (divider lines):**
- All rules are `rgba(0,0,0,0.5)` — black at 50% opacity.
- Tonal variation comes from WEIGHT, not HUE: hair (0.5px) · thin (1px) · bold (2px).
- Never colored rules. Never gradients between surfaces.

## Layout & Surfaces

- **Hairline borders** (0.5px, rgba(0,0,0,0.5)) not drop shadows.
- **No lift shadows** on non-floating surfaces. Elevation is a hairline, never a box-shadow halo.
- **Card radius:** 10px. **Button radius:** 6px. **Tile radius:** 14px. Never "rounded-2xl" or 20+px radii.
- **No rails** (left-border accent bars). No gradient backgrounds. No zebra striping. No icon gutters on lists.
- **Surface alternation** creates rhythm: white↔khaki paper. At most two paper tones per page.

## Components

**Buttons:** Helvetica. Solid red (`#CC0000`) white text for the key CTA / primary; white with 1px black border for secondary. 14px vertical / 22px horizontal padding. 6px radius. One primary per screen.

**Cards:** 0.5px hairline border, 10px radius, 24px padding, no shadow. Card text in Century. Hover darkens border from 0.5px grey to 1px black over 220ms. Khaki numeral kicker (01, 02, 03) establishes position in a set.

**Lists:** left-aligned title, right-aligned metadata, rule between rows. Three registers:
- Index: numbered, 2px top rule, 0.5px hairlines within
- Standard: un-numbered, 1px rules throughout
- Eyebrow: khaki kicker above title, 0.5px hairlines

Never bullets, checkmarks, icons in gutter, colored rules, or zebra stripes.

**Quotes:** soft paper background, 24px Century italic, 1.45 leading, attribution in Century below a hairline rule. Curly quotes, never straight.

## Illustration

**The finish:** flat vector-style editorial. Smooth simplified shapes, solid matte color, clean economical contours, minimal bounded shading. No photorealism or photorealistic detail, paper grain or noise, gradients, painterly brushwork, or cinematic lighting. "Vector-style" is a look; it doesn't require an SVG file.

**The direction:**
- **Feel:** mid-century editorial magazine illustration (1950s–60s), made modern. Calm, optimistic, intelligent, quietly confident. Print-ready, like a feature opener in a high-end international magazine such as Monocle or The New Yorker.
- **Subject:** one central figure, a thoughtful modern leader. Adult, stylized, culturally neutral, slightly elongated proportions, relaxed and confident posture. Grounded in the frame, never floating.
- **Clothing:** quiet-luxury smart casual — fine knitwear, camp-collar shirts, premium tees, tailored trousers, suede or cashmere jackets. Cardigans instead of blazers. No traditional business attire: no blazers, suits, or ties.
- **Composition:** one clear editorial metaphor, generous negative space, Swiss-grid geometry (arcs, diagonals, paths, symbolic props).
- **Palette:** muted foundation (navy, charcoal, bone, taupe, soft black, muted olive), warm mid-tones (sage, stone, light khaki, camel, faded indigo, oatmeal, espresso), and at most one or two small muted accents (rust, burgundy, dusty teal, ochre). Brand red only if it's that screen's one red moment.
- **AI, when shown:** an off-white, friendly humanoid robot with soft, gentle curves and subtle teal accents at the joints. Slightly smaller than the human figures: never the same height or bigger. A helpful collaborator, never dominant. No holograms, glowing waves, or sci-fi clichés.
- **Props, when relevant:** books, a monstera with giant leaves, a white oak desk, brass accents, a brass mushroom lamp, post-its (standard post-it colors), or a whiteboard.

**For coded work:** don't draw people or scenes as hand-built SVG. Leave a placeholder and ask me for the real image.

## Hard "Don'ts" (AI-slop tells)

- No gradient backgrounds anywhere
- No emoji in designed pieces
- No lift shadows (box-shadow halos on cards, buttons, tiles)
- No left-border accent rails on rounded containers
- No noise/grain textures
- No hand-coded SVG illustrations of people, objects, or scenes — use placeholders and ask me for real imagery
- No Helvetica Neue. No Helvetica for body text.
- No "Inter / Roboto / system sans" as the headline face
- No overused fonts (Fraunces, Playfair, generic display serifs)
- No decorative dividers, ornamental flourishes, or icon pairs
- No serif on buttons, eyebrows, or nav. No mono as prose.
- No two red moments on one screen (a key CTA button doesn't count). No khaki on khaki paper. No colored rules.
- No floating subjects (illustrations hovering without ground line / frame contact)

## When You're Unsure

- If it's signage (a header, label, button, nav) → Helvetica. If it's read (a sentence, a caption, a paragraph) → Century.
- If it's a datum (number, path, date, hex) → mono. Otherwise → not mono.
- If it's a label → khaki. If it's a statement → red (only one per screen; key CTA buttons are red too). If it's anything else → black or slate.
- When in doubt, strip decoration. The system reads by type, rule, and white space — never by fill or ornament.

## Deliverable Preferences

- 12-column grid, 20–24px gutters.
- Generous white space. Crowding is the enemy.
- When I ask for variations, give me 3+ distinct directions — not 3 color swaps of the same layout.
- Before building, confirm the voice target (stance piece? utility doc? editorial?) and which paper surface dominates.
- Always ask clarifying questions at the start of anything non-trivial.
